Free to Enter, Not Free to Race: Boarding Fees and the 30-Day Minimum

Published by the RedMagic NFT team. Not investment advice.

"Entry is free" is usually read as "racing is free." It is not. Registering a horse costs nothing, but before that horse can stand at the start line it has to be boarded at a stable run by an organizer, and boarding is paid by the day. That fee, not the entry button, is what decides whether racing is worth it for you.

The first cost: boarding

Boarding is charged per day in R-MAB and paid to the stable owner, not to the platform. The daily rate is set by the stable owner. The fact base does not record a specific figure, so I am not going to invent one — read the live rate off the stable's page.

Thirty days is a floor, not a ceiling

The rule is a minimum of 30 days per booking. You can prepay for longer, with no upper limit. It caps how short a booking can be, not how long.

For a new player the practical meaning is blunt: your first booking is a month of cost, not a one-day trial. If you only plan to race once, that month gets spread across a single race, and the effective cost per race is much higher than the daily number suggests.

It is a cost, not a deposit

Three differences worth separating:

  • Prepaid and non-refundable. If you stop racing, the unused days are not returned.
  • It does not offset payouts. It is not margin. It cannot cover race expenses, and it does not turn into extra principal when you win.
  • It does not come back when you leave. Unboarding only releases the horse from its locked state; the money has already gone to the stable owner.

A deposit is "parked here, returned when conditions are met." Boarding is "buying a block of time, used or not." Mixing the two up is how budgets go wrong.

While boarded, the horse is locked

A boarded horse cannot be sold, but it can be entered in races. The reverse is the binding rule: to enter a race, the horse must be boarded. That is the direct causal chain between "free entry" and "racing costs money."

According to the in-game docs, the boarding days that overlap with a race are paid by the race organizer, and your expiry date is extended accordingly. You also cannot unboard mid-race; the horse unlocks after settlement. The practical effect is that race days do not burn your own boarding days. It does not change the non-refundable nature of the fee itself.

Three ways the math goes wrong

  1. Treating "free entry" as "free racing" and missing the boarding layer entirely.
  2. Treating boarding as a deposit that will come back.
  3. Pricing one day and forgetting the 30-day minimum, so the real outlay is the daily rate times a month.

The full cost has at least three layers: boarding (daily, prepaid, non-refundable) + the organizer's deposit (posted before a race, topped up if short) + bet principal (which can be lost entirely). Different money, different risk. Adding them into one number understates the downside.

A question to ask before you board

Not "how much is a day," but "how long will I actually keep this horse active?" If the answer is less than a month, the 30-day minimum is the number that matters. If the answer is longer, prepaying for more days is allowed and there is no cap on how far ahead you can go.

All of this is in the twelve in-game documentation sections and can be checked line by line. The daily rate should be read from the stable page itself.
