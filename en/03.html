Fourteen Contracts, a Four-Tier Split, and a Future Block Hash: Inside Red Veil's On-Chain Structure

Personal notes, not an official announcement. Not investment advice.

Published by the official RedMagic NFT team. The only live site is https://redveil.redmagic.pro.

This one is for people who would rather check the chain than read a pitch. Every address below comes from the site's single configuration file and matches what the interface actually calls.

Network

BNB Smart Chain mainnet, chainId 56, RPC https://bsc-dataseed1.bnbchain.org. Three fallback nodes are configured; the app rotates when the primary is rate-limited.

Three separate accounting lines

One: the betting pool. Fixed stake of 1,000 R-MAB or 1 USDT per bet. The pool splits 100% into 75% to backers of the winner, 5% rebate to horse owners by volume, 20% to the platform.

Two: the four-tier split. That 20% — along with the platform's cut of market tax and stud fees — is divided as treasury 30%, proposer 30%, inviter 30%, foundation senior 10%. The ratios live in contract storage and are owner-adjustable.

Three: trade tax. A marketplace sale takes 20%, the seller nets 80%, and the full tax amount flows through the four tiers.

Keeping these separate matters: payout money is never used to fund the split, and split money is never used to top up a prize pool.

How a result is produced

At the start of a race, the contract snapshots every horse's stats — gene, training, bloodline, wins, level. The result is then computed from a block hash generated after the start, combined with the snapshot hash.

The word that matters is "after". At the moment the race begins, the hash does not exist yet, so neither the host nor the platform can know the outcome in advance, and there is no ordering to roll back.

The beta boundary

To be explicit: this is a public beta, and the contracts retain admin rights. That means parameters, fees, and pause switches can change — and it means "fully decentralized" is not a claim we make, or intend to make.

Two things we can stand behind: the addresses above are the ones the page reads, and when the page and the chain disagree, the chain is correct.
